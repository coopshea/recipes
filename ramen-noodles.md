# Ramen Noodles (Machine Rolled)

Makes 2 servings. Adapted from the noodle-making class by The Story of Ramen.

**Make batches of 2 servings at most.** At ~39% hydration the dough is too dry to mix evenly in larger amounts. For more servings, mix several batches separately.

## Ingredients

| Ingredient               | Grams |
|--------------------------|------:|
| Bread flour              |   180 |
| Water, room temp         |    64 |
| Lye water (kansui)       |     6 |
| Fine sea salt            |     2 |

Hydration: 70 g liquid / 180 g flour ≈ 39%.

## Method

1. Weigh the bread flour into a prep bowl.
2. Weigh the water into a second bowl. Add the salt and lye water and stir until the salt dissolves.
3. Pour the liquid into the flour gradually while stirring with chopsticks.
4. Keep stirring until all the flour is moistened. Break up large clumps into small crumbs.
5. Squeeze the crumbs into a ball by hand and turn out onto a work surface. Divide into individual servings. A full batch is too much for a pasta roller at once.
6. Flatten each piece as much as you can. Pass it through the roller once on the thickest setting (#1).
7. Fold the sheet in half and feed it back through with the fold at a right angle to the rollers. Repeat until smooth. KitchenAid roller: 7 passes total.
8. Roll thinner without folding. KitchenAid roller: one pass each on settings 2, 3, and 4.
9. Cut with the spaghetti attachment.

## Mixing tips

- **Food processor:** pulse the flour, then stream the liquid in with the motor running. Process 30–60 s until it looks like damp, even crumbs that clump when pinched.
- **Stand mixer, paddle:** low speed, add liquid in 3 parts, mix 3–5 min. The paddle works better than the hook on dough this dry.
- **Hand rub:** after the chopsticks, rub the crumbs between your palms like pie dough to spread the moisture.
- **Rest before rolling:** bag the crumbs, press into a slab, and rest 30 min. The flour hydrates and the dough comes together much more easily. Stepping on the bagged dough (wrapped in a towel) is the traditional way to knead it.
